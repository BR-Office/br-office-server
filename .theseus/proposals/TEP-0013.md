## Problem
After two rounds, the time to open a Word document did not move (about 3.5 s for 10 pages, 4.2 s for 100 pages, the same as the first measurement). Each module's diagnosis looked only inside its own module and picked its hottest function; no one owned what users do from end to end. Opening goes through five modules (server, converter, editor scripts, UI build, layout and first paint); each improved a piece of its own, and the sum, which is what the user feels, stayed the same. Rafael: "if it is complete optimization, analyze the whole application, break it into modules and functions, and do not pick one or another."

## Proposed change
`goals.journeys` in the adapter names what users do, each with a command that measures it end to end against the integrated build. Each round starts by mapping every journey into stages down to module, file and function, with the time of each, adding up to the total. Every module is then diagnosed against the maps and lists every stage it is given, in `covers` or `not_covered` with a reason. After every integrated change, every journey is measured and recorded in `.theseus/scoreboard/journeys.jsonl`. (Theseus 0.24.0.)

## Hypothesis
"Every stage of every journey map has an owner in a diagnosis (validate passes with coverage), and the open of a 10-page document falls below 2.5 s (p50, integrated build) within the next 40 records, with no differential-oracle regression."

## How to verify
`theseus validate` coverage errors at 0 after the round's diagnoses; `.theseus/scoreboard/journeys.jsonl` for `open-docx-10p` from its first record to the 40th later record; differential oracles all PASS.

## Risks
Mapping and re-diagnosing every round costs more per round; journey measurements are noisy on this 2-CPU machine (identical code differs by up to ±700 ms on open), so a gain must exceed the recorded min–max spread.
